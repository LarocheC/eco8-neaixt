# Paper audit

Auditing a draft against evidence. The draft's persuasiveness is not evidence;
neither is the fact that it was written by someone who ran the experiments.

Autonomous manuscript generation has a poor track record — independent
evaluation of the original "AI Scientist" found failed experiments, missing
baselines, false novelty assessments and manuscript numbers that did not match
the runs. Evidence assembly is worth automating hard. Novelty and interpretation
stay human-gated.

## Pass 1 — every number

For each number in the draft: find the artifact it came from.

- Trace it to a file under `research/experiments/` or `benchmarks/`, or to a
  generated table. **A number that only appears in prose is a finding, not a
  fact** — list it.
- Check it was not hand-copied: is the table generated by a script from a
  committed artifact (`benchmarks/report.py` is the reference)?
- Check for staleness: does the number predate a correction? Both corrections on
  record — the Einsum int8 re-measurement and the `monarch`→`blockdiag`
  rename — invalidated previously published values.

## Pass 2 — every claim

For each claim, find its `research/CLAIMS.yaml` entry. If there is none, the
claim is unaudited; flag it.

Then check the draft does not exceed the entry's `status` and `scope`:

| entry says | draft may not say |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| `compute-only` | anything about latency, energy or speed |
| `pilot` | anything general; it is one seed or a subset |
| `unverified` | anything at all, in a paper |
| `corrected` | the pre-correction number |
| scope: one target | "on embedded hardware" in general |
| scope: latency only | anything about energy |

## Pass 3 — the missing pieces

- **Matched baselines.** Is every efficiency claim compared against a
  compute-matched static, not only against the largest model?
- **Split hygiene.** Does the paper describe how architectures, checkpoints,
  thresholds and calibration sets were chosen? If it does not say, assume the
  worst and ask.
- **Metric consistency.** Same harness, same versions, same dataset revision
  across every compared row. SCOREQ full-reference distance is lower-better;
  check the direction of every reported delta.
- **Negative results.** Are the failures in `research/FAILURES.md` represented?
  A paper that reports only what worked is describing a different project.
- **Citations.** Every reference checked against the source, not against
  memory of it — does the cited paper actually say what it is cited for?
- **Novelty.** Do not assess it. Flag every novelty phrase ("first", "novel",
  "unlike prior work") for the human.

## Output

A table: `claim in draft | evidence | verdict | what to change`. Verdicts:
`supported`, `overstated`, `unsupported`, `stale`, `untraceable`.

Do not rewrite the draft unless asked. Report first.
